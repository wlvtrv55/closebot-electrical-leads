# ai appointment setter for electricians: CloseBot pricing, setup, and which electrical leads text can actually book

It's 2:40 on a Tuesday. You're in a crawlspace with a panel cover in one hand, and your phone is buzzing in the truck. By the time you climb out, the homeowner with the dead circuit has already called two other electricians, and one of them picked up.

That's the real problem an AI appointment setter is supposed to solve — not "customer service," not "engagement," but the gap between a lead arriving and someone replying. And per industry data compiled from NECA member surveys, electrical contractors miss roughly 23% of inbound calls, with around 58% arriving outside normal business hours.

So the question isn't whether you're losing leads. It's whether software can actually answer them the way an electrical job needs to be answered. CloseBot is one of the tools that comes up repeatedly in that conversation, and it has a specific shape: it's text-first, CRM-native, and priced by message volume. Whether that fits an electrical business depends on details most landing pages skip.

## What an AI appointment setter actually does in an electrical business

Strip away the marketing and there are five jobs:

1. **Reply immediately** — seconds, not hours, on whatever channel the lead used.
2. **Qualify** — pull the details a tech needs before rolling a truck.
3. **Route urgency** — separate "sparks and burning smell" from "quote for a garage sub-panel."
4. **Book** — put a real slot on the calendar instead of promising a callback.
5. **Follow up** — confirm, remind, and reschedule when someone cancels.

Steps 2 through 5 are where generic chatbots fall apart in this trade. An electrical lead needs context: is this residential or commercial, is the panel 100-amp or 200-amp, is a permit involved, is there attic or crawlspace access, is the driveway long enough that the quote changes, and is the homeowner actually the decision-maker or a tenant.

Those fields matter for two reasons. First, they filter out the tire-kickers before they eat a slot on your calendar. Second, they surface the upsell before anyone drives out — a homeowner asking about a Level 2 EV charger is frequently also a panel upgrade, and that conversation is much cheaper to have in a text thread than on a second site visit.

Tools built specifically for home services ship these as native capabilities. CloseBot, for example, includes a distance check between locations (useful for drive-time gating), US property data, and property images via aerial and street view on its home services page. That's a step above a generic chatbot that only knows what you typed into a knowledge base.

### The part most tool pages skip: most electrician leads arrive as phone calls

Here's the honest limitation. CloseBot is text-only. Its own homepage describes it as taking over "all text-based channels within your CRM." No voice agent, no phone answering — voice is a hole in the product, and third-party comparisons list it that way.

That matters more for electricians than for, say, a coaching business, because calls still dominate this trade. Industry surveys cited by answering-service vendors put "customers who prefer booking by phone" at roughly two-thirds, and one HomeAdvisor contractor study suggests the first contractor to answer captures close to 78% of emergency jobs.

So the accurate framing isn't "CloseBot answers your phone." It's "CloseBot handles the text half of your lead flow so the phone half isn't the only thing standing between you and a booked job." If your leads come from Google Ads forms, website chat widgets, Facebook lead forms, or SMS — which is most of what an electrician's paid traffic produces — that text half is bigger than it sounds. If your funnel is 100% "call now" buttons, this is the wrong category of tool and you'd be shopping for voice AI instead.

## Where CloseBot actually sits in an electrical lead flow

CloseBot builds AI agents that qualify leads, follow up, and book appointments. You connect it to a CRM — GoHighLevel and HubSpot are native, and there's a "custom CRM" path plus a connector system — and the agent then replies inside the text channels that CRM already handles: SMS, website live chat, email, and social DMs routed through that inbox.

A few specifics worth knowing before you compare it to anything else:

- **It's agentic, not a flow chart.** You set an objective and give the agent knowledge and tools; it reasons through the conversation rather than following button trees. In practice that means you don't have to pre-script every possible phrasing of "my breaker keeps tripping."
- **It can run with no CRM at all.** CloseBot's own home services FAQ says you can use CloseBot alone to drop a chat widget on your website that replies to leads, books jobs, and quotes. Connecting a CRM adds pipelines and task management, not the ability to chat.
- **There's a testing portal and a human takeover.** You can test conversations before going live, roll back changes, and pause the AI on a single conversation when a real person needs to step in.
- **Smart FAQ escalates instead of guessing.** When the agent hits a question it can't answer confidently, it flags it to you — and then re-engages every lead who asked that question once you've answered it. In a trade where a wrong answer about permit timelines or panel capacity has real cost, "I'll flag this" is a better failure mode than a confident hallucination.
- **You can pick the model provider per persona,** with automatic fallback if your primary provider fails. That's a reliability feature, not a marketing one: sole reliance on a single API is how a Saturday-morning lead goes unanswered.
- **Language coverage** is claimed at 40+ languages on CloseBot's site, while a third-party listing notes English only. If you serve a bilingual market, ask before you buy rather than trusting either line.

### The costs that sit outside the subscription

Three add-ons show up after the base price:

- **Additional user seats:** $5 each per month. Useful if your office manager needs to see conversations, or if a client wants portal access.
- **Extra knowledge storage:** business plans include 1 MB and add-ons run from $0.10 to $3.00 per MB per month depending on volume. For context, 1 MB of text is roughly 1,000 pages — for a single electrical company, this barely registers. For an agency uploading price books for twenty clients, it does.
- **Message overage:** business plans bill overages at 2x the per-message rate, drawn from a wallet you top up. The agency plan bills $0.012 per message, rebillable at whatever markup you set.

One definition worth pinning down before you budget: a message equals a segment, *unless* you enable the Agent Node's "unlimited potential" (many tools, unlimited instruction size), at which point you're billed token costs and a single message can consume several segments.

> **One thing to confirm directly with support:** CloseBot's help documentation states that V2 requires you to use your own AI provider API keys and that token costs aren't covered, while the pricing page's FAQ says the opposite — that "bring your own key" is not allowed for security reasons, with model spend baked into the plan. Those two statements can't both be true, and the difference affects your monthly bill. Ask before you commit, and read the invoice on the free plan for a month first.

Also worth knowing: **there are no refunds.** CloseBot compensates with a genuinely free tier and a 7-day trial of any paid plan before billing begins, on month-to-month terms with no contract. That trial window is where your testing has to happen.

## What that looks like at electrician-sized volume

The arithmetic matters more than the tier names here.

**Scenario 1 — one van, light volume.** You get 25 leads a month, each needing about 8–12 messages of back-and-forth. That's roughly 250 messages, comfortably over the free plan's 100. You'd either pay $0.08 per message for the extra 150 ($12/month) or move to the $64 business plan with its 500 included messages. If a single booked panel job is worth several hundred dollars, the $12 version is a legitimate first experiment, and the $64 version is cheaper than the free plan the moment your volume creeps up.

**Scenario 2 — a service company with real ad spend.** Say you're pushing 3,000 messages a month. On the agency plan that's $397 plus about $36 in message costs at $0.012 — and that $36 is rebillable if you're operating as an agency. On the business track, you'd be raising your monthly message ceiling to take advantage of bulk pricing rather than paying overage rates.

The point isn't that these numbers are small. It's that the base price isn't the bill, and the bill scales with conversations, not with booked jobs. Model it against your actual message volume before you decide.

## CRM or no CRM — the decision that sets your real cost

CloseBot is built to live inside a CRM. In practice that means your total monthly outlay often looks like this:

- CloseBot business plan: from $64/month
- GoHighLevel: about $97/month for the entry tier, around $297 for the higher tier, based on third-party pricing breakdowns (GoHighLevel is a separate subscription you'd pay regardless of which AI tool you use)

So a solo electrician with no CRM is looking at roughly $160–$360/month before usage, or $64–$176 on the business track if they only use the free-standing chat widget and skip the CRM.

That's the structural trade-off, and it's the same one a contractor-focused comparison made bluntly: tools built on top of GoHighLevel are agency tools first, and the economics make more sense when you're managing twenty client accounts than when you're a single company trying to follow up on a few hundred leads.

The flip side is real too. If you already run GoHighLevel or HubSpot, CloseBot is a focused upgrade to whatever conversational AI came bundled with it, and the agency plan's white-label portal and rebilling turn the subscription into a revenue line rather than a cost.

**A gap to check before you buy:** electricians usually live in ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, or FieldEdge. CloseBot books through CRM calendar integrations — GoHighLevel, HubSpot, LeadConnector, or a custom stack — not natively into field service software. If your scheduling lives in one of those platforms, confirm the path from booked appointment to your dispatch board with support during the trial. It's a solvable problem, but it's not a checkbox on the pricing page.

## Who this fits, and who it doesn't

**A reasonable fit if you:**

- Generate leads through website forms, chat, paid social, or SMS — anything that lands as a text conversation.
- Already run GoHighLevel or HubSpot, or are willing to add one.
- Want the same qualification logic applied consistently on a Sunday night as on a Wednesday morning.
- Care about the follow-up sequence more than the first reply — most of the revenue in this trade sits in reminders, reschedules, and second attempts at people who went quiet.

**Probably not the right tool if you:**

- Get nearly all your work from inbound phone calls and want the phone answered.
- Sell big-ticket commercial projects through long consultative cycles rather than booked site visits.
- Want one flat bill with nothing metered underneath.
- Need field-service dispatch integration on day one with no customization.

Reader reviews land in roughly the same place. G2 reviewers praise the conversation quality and note that multi-agent tag-switching gets fiddly on complex handovers, and contractors on the pricing question point out that quality depends heavily on how well you configure the pipeline and follow-up logic — one G2 reviewer put it as the AI scaling your sloppiness faster if the offer or follow-up is messy. On r/automation, a critic described the workflow builder concept as good but the testing and live experience as less reliable than they'd like.

None of that is a dealbreaker. It does mean the tool rewards the operator who actually finishes the setup.

## FAQ

**Will an AI appointment setter replace my office manager?**
No, and vendors who imply otherwise are overselling. CloseBot's own position is that the agent qualifies and books, while scheduling, invoicing, and anything requiring judgment stays human. The realistic gain is that your office manager stops chasing cold leads and starts handling paid work.

**Does CloseBot answer phone calls?**
No. It handles text-based channels — SMS, website chat, email, and social channels routed through your CRM. For voice, you need a separate voice AI tool, and using both is common.

**How fast does it reply?**
CloseBot markets second-level response times on inbound text conversations. The practical advantage over a human team isn't the seconds — it's the consistency at 9 p.m. on a Sunday.

**What happens when the agent doesn't know something?**
Smart FAQ flags the question to you instead of inventing an answer, then follows up with every lead who asked once you've supplied it. For electrical work, that's the right behavior: a hallucinated permit timeline costs more than a delayed reply.

**Is there a contract?**
No. Plans are month to month, with a free tier and a 7-day trial of paid plans. But there are no refunds, so the trial is your only safety net — use it like one.
